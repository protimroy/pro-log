Fit six-history interpolated Kneser–Ney using training counts and R10's fixed discount 0.95. Retain every one- and two-character observed suffix, then count the remaining gates. For observed suffixes of lengths three to six, rank by training frequency, by frequency times the KL divergence between the suffix distribution and its immediate shorter suffix, and by that KL score multiplied by N/(N+4) to shrink low-support candidates. Keep the same number of suffixes for each ranking. For every retained suffix store its log probability vector minus that of the nearest retained proper suffix. Match the longest retained suffix by summing the contributions of all matching gates. Choose the best ranking by validation loss and evaluate held-out loss only after fixing the selection.
